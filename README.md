### Summary of the artefact: My Personal Reflection

The main lesson learned from the workshop's theory was the classification of AI, which is divided into three types: ANI (narrow AI, which exists today), AGI (general AI, capable of planning and making decisions like a human), and ASI (superAI, which will surpass humanity). I hadn't come across any specific specifications before. It was also interesting to hear from other class members how the older generation and vulnerable groups with mental health issues can perceive AI as thinking, feeling, and empathising, although all it can do is mimic understanding and feelings, as I come from a tech family where everyone thinks of it as an unspoken truth.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I tried to capture two moments simultaneously when I took this screenshot: my chat message about the leaked private police video incidents, and the end of the workshop, where you can clearly see the happy faces of me, the tutor, and the rest of the class. I captured this moment in chat because AI training and content generation based on illegally obtained and unlicensed data is one of the main topics I keep in mind at night. This is a very complex legal framework that still lacks a clear legal basis. Unlike standard copyright, where it's easy to catch an infringer using it illegally, when training AI on copyrighted data, you need to request access to the internals of the language models. When generating content based on illegally obtained images, things are even worse. I have a personal view of news stories like Artifact 2 and the general idea of ​​generating content based on publicly available information, as I have social media myself, my sister has social media, and my future little brother will be born into a world where anyone can use a photo from his school soccer tournament for completely illegal purposes. In such cases, it's difficult to catch the perpetrator, as the content can be created locally, distributed in private chats or via disappearing messages, and leave no obvious traces of the source data and tools used. Without access to devices, accounts, and the internal logs of an AI service, proving the origin of an image and establishing responsibility can be nearly impossible. It turns out that the technical ability to cause harm is developing faster than the mechanisms for investigation and protection of victims. This workshop once again made me think about what AI is capable of creating, and whether there is such a thing as ethical use of AI, without the legal workframe that violates freedome of speach through limitations of any kind.
