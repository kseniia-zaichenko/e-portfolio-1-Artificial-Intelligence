### Summary of the artefact: My Personal Reflection

The main lesson learned from the workshop's theory was the classification of AI, which is divided into three types: ANI (narrow AI, which exists today), AGI (general AI, capable of planning and making decisions like a human), and ASI (superAI, which will surpass humanity). I hadn't come across any specific specifications before. It was also interesting to hear from other class members how the older generation and vulnerable groups with mental health issues can perceive AI as thinking, feeling, and empathising, although all it can do is mimic understanding and feelings, as I come from a tech family where everyone thinks of it as an unspoken truth.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

The screenshot captures two moments simultaneously: my chat message about the leaked private police body-camera videos on YouTube and the workshop’s end as evidence of attendance. I included this message in chat because AI training and content generation relying on illegally obtained or unlicensed data keep me up at night. This is an incredibly complex legal area that still lacks clear guidelines. Unlike standard copyright, where it's easier to identify the copied work and how it was used, identifying AI that was trained is much more complicated, as datasets, internal records, and model processes are inaccessible for publicity. When generating content based on illegally obtained images, things are even worse. Thus, the South Australian case discussed in Artefact 2 shows a teenager pleaded guilty to creating or altering sexually explicit deepfakes and using an online service to distribute them, making the fears part of modern everyday life (Blandis 2026). Similar content can be created locally, distributed in private chats or via disappearing messages, and leave no obvious traces of the source data and tools used. Without access to devices, accounts, and the internal logs of an AI service, proving the origin of an image and establishing responsibility can be nearly impossible. This workshop once again made me think about what AI is capable of creating and whether there is such a thing as ethical use of AI, without a legal framework that violates freedom of speech through limitations of any kind.
