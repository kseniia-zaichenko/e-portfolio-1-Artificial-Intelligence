### Summary of the artefact: My Personal Reflection

The main lesson learned from the workshop's theory was the classification of AI, which is divided into three types: ANI (narrow AI, which exists today), AGI (general AI, capable of planning and making decisions like a human), and ASI (superAI, which will surpass humanity). I hadn't come across any specific specifications before. It was also interesting to hear from other class members how the older generation and vulnerable groups with mental health issues can perceive AI as thinking, feeling, and empathising, although all it can do is mimic understanding and feelings, as I come from a tech family where everyone thinks of it as an unspoken truth.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

The screenshot captures two moments simultaneously: my chat message about the leaked police body-camera videos on YouTube, which could be misused unethically in the context of AI, and the workshop’s end as proof of attendance. I included this message in chat because AI training and content generation relying on illegally obtained or unlicensed data keep me up at night. This is an incredibly complex legal area that still lacks clear guidelines. Unlike standard copyright, where copying is easier to identify, tracing AI training data is hard since datasets and model processes are private. Content generated from illegal images is even worse. Thus, the South Australian case discussed earlier shows a teenager pleaded guilty to creating and sharing sexually explicit deepfakes and using an online service to distribute them, making illegal AI content part of modern life (Blandis 2026). Similar content can be created locally, distributed in private chats or via disappearing messages, and leave no obvious traces of the source data and tools used. Without access to devices, accounts, and the internal logs of an AI service, proving the origin of an image and establishing responsibility can be nearly impossible. This workshop once again made me think about what AI is capable of creating and if there’s an ethical way to use it without a legal framework that violates freedom of speech through limitations of any kind.
